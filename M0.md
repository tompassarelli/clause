# Clause M0 evidence contract

## Constitution, contrast corpus, and parser gate

**Status:** distinction constitution and indentation ruling; no post-M0
milestone begins until this contract and corpus are reviewed and public

Clause has one semantic domain of addressable referents. A referent is a
stabilized distinction reidentifiable across relational content, assertion
occurrences, and Revisions and is not collapsed by structural equality.
Relations are addressable referents in relational position. N-ary relational
content assigns each participant to a stable named role; an assertion
occurrence is the scoped act that commits to that content, and judgment remains
separate from both. Terms denote referents but are not the referents. Universal
law, oriented derivation rule, Revision-admission invariant, and goal are
distinct modes.

**Authority:** [FOUNDATION.md](FOUNDATION.md) is the semantic authority,
[ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) is the milestone authority, and
[SURFACE.md](SURFACE.md) owns human-surface projection. This contract freezes
executable evidence for the distinction constitution, classification and
definition syntax, and indentation while retaining a strict distinction
between target authority and the current executable profile.

## 1. What this checkpoint fixes

The following are directional invariants:

- source terms denote addressable referents rather than creating primitive
  types, values, objects, records, fields, or variables;
- a declaration position may establish a referent without automatically
  establishing roles or constraints; a use position resolves an existing
  referent unless explicitly fresh or variable;
- relational content, assertion occurrence, and judgment remain separate, and
  two occurrences may share one content identity while preserving source and
  provenance;
- absence remains undetermined under the open world and never becomes denial;
- universal law, oriented derivation rule, Revision-admission invariant, and
  goal remain distinct and confer none of one another's authority;
- a focused block is erasable projection over semantically distinct focused
  forms; membership and other relational content remain non-owned and co-equal, while
  definitions remain distinct from relational content;
- the focused `state := locked` line elaborates to the definition
  `state of focus := locked`, distinct from object fields and relational content;
- `x : Group` is classification sugar for an ordinary membership relation;
- `name := term` is definition/denotation, not classification or equality;
- `∈`, `::`, and `in` are not canonical membership source, and no editor
  rewrites input to `∈`;
- formatters and agents emit `:` classification and `:=` definition directly;
- strong, unambiguous conventional relations use canonical ASCII operators
  such as `>`, `<`, `>=`, `<=`, `=`, and spaced infix `+`, `-`, and `*`, while
  every such form still elaborates to a relation referent with stable named
  roles;
- word-expanded operator source such as `greater-than` is noncanonical, while
  domain relations such as `connects` and `parent-of` remain words;
- structurally leading `+` and `-` remain exact Delta signs, and slash-qualified
  semantic names are never reinterpreted as arithmetic;
- canonical indentation is two spaces per level, spaces only, and tabs are
  diagnosed;
- enumeration, homogeneous definition/shape, and focused blocks are
  distinct forms;
- the lossless reader is semantic-free, while role-labelled elaboration occurs
  in a later stage; and
- the existing Rust grammar and semantic core remain migration/parity evidence.

Focused definition is settled: a `name := value` child defines `name of focus`.
It cannot alter classification, indentation, or ordinary relational-content and
assertion-occurrence semantics.

Current executable-profile request headers that contain the word `in` remain
migration evidence; that phrase position is not a membership operator and does
not create a target membership alias.

The current Rust frontend, kernel, semantic-v5 wire, and fixtures use separate
`Type`, `Entity`, `Value`, `Variable`, and `Relation` representations. They are
sealed executable migration evidence, not the constitutional Model. M0 neither
redesigns their wire identity nor presents them as proof that those views are
primitive. A later migration must project them from referents, named-role
relational content, assertion occurrences and judgments, and explicit modes
while preserving Revision lineage, provenance, explanation, intervention,
exact identity, and source-deleted executable parity.

Clause contains no Store implementation and M0 asserts no Store closure. Store
is a neutral persistence and query substrate, not semantic authority. Any
future adapter requires a typed Clause envelope for stable referent terms,
relational content, assertion-occurrence attestations, judgments, modality,
evidence, authority, admission/rejection, supersession, and exact
Clause-Revision-to-storage-lineage links. It may infer none from structural
equality, row liveness, missing rows, retraction, query negation, or storage
revision identity.

Any future Store vocabulary must also treat types, values, data, objects,
fields, records, sets, functions, variables, state, mutation, checking, and
evaluation only as derived relational views. Sets are membership plus optional
extensionality, functions are relations plus uniqueness/orientation, and type
checking is a relational admissibility proof. Representations become explicit
only when relevant.

Relations, rules, Revisions, and evaluation may themselves be discussed as
referents, but mention never executes them. Relation, rule, effect, or evaluator
interpretation requires an admitted shape and mode and may require quotation,
stratification, or an exact Revision boundary.

## 2. Corpus contract

The paired files under `clause:corpus/m0/cases/` use:

```text
CASE.clause
CASE.expected.json
```

Each JSON document declares `clause-m0-contrast-v1` and records:

- exact source lines and line-break policy, sufficient to reconstruct the
  source bytes;
- the semantic or structural axis under test;
- fixed requirements and forbidden readings;
- accepted, rejected, unresolved, or implemented-legacy decision metadata; and
- an abstract role-labelled elaboration where the semantics are fixed.

Abstract relation handles begin with `$`. They name semantic roles without
choosing a surface spelling or inventing a Store identity. Two cases in the
same `equivalence_group` must have identical `elaborated` values whenever
that field is present.

An unresolved case is evidence input, not an accepted program. Its oracle says,
“if this candidate is admitted, these invariants must hold.” A later M0 ruling
must retain the case and add the measured verdict; it may not silently bless or
delete the losing contrast.

## 3. Reader and stage boundary

The eventual pipeline remains:

```text
UTF-8 bytes
  -> editor/input validation (no membership rewrite)
  -> Stage A: lossless concrete tree + source/layout diagnostics
  -> Stage B: structural block classes + classification diagnostics
  -> Stage C: exact scoped phrase and recursive-term resolution
  -> Stage D: role-labelled authored judgments
  -> current checked semantic core where honest
```

The editor boundary is not grammar: it preserves source and reports retired
spellings rather than manufacturing `∈`. Stage A retains every source byte,
line break, delimiter, token, trivia range, and diagnostic without deciding
object, type, membership, relation, law, rule, invariant, goal, judgment, or
query meaning. It groups canonical two-space indentation, diagnoses tabs and
noncanonical indentation, and does not silently normalize them. Stage B
classifies structural modes from that group tree: relational content/assertion
occurrence, relation contract, definition, universal law, oriented derivation
rule, invariant, query, goal, observation, requirement, intention, effect,
transition, and delta remain distinct wherever provable. If Stage B cannot
prove a category, it emits an explicit `Unresolved...` class for later
resolution rather than accepting a collapsed union. Stage C never uses
probabilistic NLP.

## 4. Precedent boundary

The conventional components are documented by primary sources:

- [Python indentation stacks](https://docs.python.org/3/reference/lexical_analysis.html#indentation);
- [Haskell layout expansion](https://www.haskell.org/onlinereport/lexemes.html#sect2.7);
- [Agda mixfix and fixity resolution](https://agda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/language/mixfix-operators.html);
- [rust-analyzer's full-fidelity syntax trees](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust-analyzer/blob/master/docs/book/src/contributing/syntax.md); and
- [rustc paired expected-output tests](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/tests/compiletest.html).

Clause's decision is not to invent those mechanisms. The novelty under test is
their composition with form-inferred blocks, exact recursive phrase shapes,
erasable focus, and stable role-labelled semantics.

## 5. Retained contrast sets

### 5.1 Foundational surface set

The first set contains:

- the controlling mixed-form focus specimen at canonical two spaces and a
  rejected noncanonical four-space rendering;
- an accepted expanded equivalent using `:` classification plus retired
  `member of`, `∈`, `::`, and `in` membership spellings;
- canonical two-space enumeration and rejected four-space enumeration;
- a tab-indented source with the required layout diagnostic;
- a homogeneous derived value-shape classification block;
- `thing : Space`, which is ordinary membership rather than definition; and
- focused `state := locked`, which defines the stable `state of focus` term and
  is forbidden from becoming an object field or ordinary `state` relational content.

### 5.2 Relation-shape set

The second set adds:

- a compact `connects` schema candidate with three named, domain-bound roles
  and one operational projection/cardinality contract;
- a focused functional `position` contract that remains a relation contract,
  never a field declaration;
- focused, expanded, and explicit structural renderings of one `connects`
  relational content item with an identical role graph;
- one deliberately overlapping pair of exact phrase shapes whose diagnostic
  must name both candidates and the conflicting focus-role domains; and
- one root fragment missing the required focus role, which must diagnose that
  role rather than invent an implicit object.

Schema, arrow, cardinality, and structural-escape spellings remain candidates.
Their role names, participant domains, projection orientation, co-equal relational content
semantics, and exact ambiguity obligations are the evidence fixed here.

### 5.3 Recursive-term set

The third set adds:

- a `:=` pure distance definition whose pattern and body contain named
  holes and nested projection terms;
- a definition whose denotation is a one-valued relation projection;
- a relational-content item whose destination role contains a grouped addition of two
  projections;
- grouped and ungrouped overlap expressions that reach the same checked tree
  only because domain checking rejects the alternate grouping; and
- left-grouped, right-grouped, and ungrouped mixfix terms where both groupings
  remain type-correct, so the ungrouped form must diagnose ambiguity.

This set does not publish a global precedence table. Parentheses are retained
in canonical evidence, every eligible role contains a recursive term tree, and
an ungrouped form is accepted only when one checked elaboration survives.

### 5.4 Holes, derivation rules, and selection set

The fourth set adds:

- naked clauses with anonymous, named, and multiple named holes;
- two anonymous holes in one clause, which must receive distinct identities;
- a multi-clause `select` whose repeated named hole correlates the clauses but
  is not projected;
- `any` existence, exact-one selection, and canonical-first selection as three
  distinct result contracts;
- an `if`-oriented positive derivation rule with repeated named holes across conclusion
  and premises; and
- the same semantic derivation rule with an optional `:=` human label
  definition that does not replace its stable hidden identity.

Expected rows use one explicit finite fact context and canonical ordering. They
are semantic oracles, not permission for universal or unbounded logic search.
Random witness choice is absent from every pure query judgment.

### 5.5 Revision ancestry and signed-delta set

The fifth set adds:

- a successor Revision with one exact withdrawal in the reset surface;
- a renamed human header with the same base, Delta, and semantic Revision;
- the current `Revision`/`from:`/`withdraw:` profile as migration-parity
  evidence, not a target candidate;
- reset and current-profile forms of one exact admission;
- one successor containing both an admission and a withdrawal;
- exact diagnostics for an unknown base, nonexistent withdrawal, existing
  admission, and overlapping signed changes; and
- a forward `diff base -> successor` request whose resolved Revision identities
  and authored assertion difference preserve direction.

Signed changes are atomic and ground. The base is an exact Revision identity,
the human header is a separate definition, and equivalent direct/current-profile
forms produce the same content-derived successor identity. These fixtures do
not admit a particular header, sign, arrow, or indentation spelling.

### 5.6 Explanation, intervention, and semantic-diff set

The sixth set adds:

- reset and current-profile forms of one Revision-scoped explanation query;
- a complete frontier of two inclusion-minimal supports with retained proof
  structure;
- reset and current-profile forms of complete `prevent all minimal` and
  `achieve all minimal` intervention queries over one named finite relation
  basis;
- explicit candidate-bound exhaustion results that retain only individually
  certified interventions and never certify a complete frontier; and
- a layered semantic diff separating authored assertion, entailment, selected
  proof, and minimal-support changes across exact Revisions.

Explanation and intervention spellings remain candidates. Fixed here are
Revision scope, proof/support identity, inclusion-minimality, typed finite
bases, deterministic prefixes, honest completeness, and diff-layer separation.

### 5.7 Transition and deterministic-replay set

The seventh set adds:

- complete-relational-content `~>` succession for a checker-proved functional `position`
  relation and complete-definition succession for a stable focused `state` term;
- exact `+` admission and `-` withdrawal for a multi-valued relation under
  event scopes;
- two guarded functional replacements that match one exact pre-state and
  commit together as one successor;
- both source orders of competing writes to the same functional key, which
  must produce the same conflict rather than selecting a winner;
- rejection of `~>` when no functional key has been proved, with repair through
  exact signed deltas or a proved functional contract; and
- deterministic replay from an exact Model Revision and initial State
  authority, a fixed tick policy, and one ordered event stream, including
  byte-identical canonical successor evidence.

Transition spellings remain candidates. Fixed here are pre-state matching,
atomic successor commit, complete-form replacement, exact multi-valued
deltas, functional-key proof, conflict rejection independent of source order,
and replay identity. This set does not select phase or merge syntax,
persistence policy, or a general replay surface; the following evidence
separately fixes only the default post-commit effect and receipt boundaries.

### 5.8 Requirements, effects, receipts, and resources set

The eighth set adds:

- a reserved `requires` list whose bare children are exact package
  requirements of the current Model/program rather than domain membership;
- a capability-requirement entry that remains distinct from runtime capability
  authorization;
- unnamed and `:=`-defined `render! scene` forms with one identical effect
  request and receipt, where definition only names the result;
- `load!` returning an opaque session-scoped resource handle while the effect
  attempt still produces a receipt;
- capability denial before any host attempt or receipt is fabricated; and
- successful and failed post-commit rendering after one identical atomic state
  successor, both observing the post-state and neither changing State identity.

Effect spellings and capability-entry syntax remain candidates. Fixed here are
Model requirement identity, request/authorization/attempt/receipt separation,
opaque resource provenance and lifetime, post-commit observation, failure
evidence, and the rule that receipts are not admitted truth. This set does not
select adapter APIs, host scheduling, async syntax, retry policy, JavaScript or
Three.js bindings, resource persistence, or a canonical capability spelling.

### 5.9 Foundational distinction set

The ninth set adds exact executable contrasts proving:

- a declaration may establish a referent without automatically creating roles
  or constraints;
- a misspelled use remains unresolved and never creates a fresh identity;
- two assertion occurrences may share one relational-content identity while
  retaining distinct source/provenance coordinates;
- absence is undetermined rather than denial;
- an oriented derivation rule adds a supported consequence while an invariant
  independently rejects admission of a violating candidate Revision;
- a goal does not assert current truth or authorize derivation;
- rename preserves ReferentId;
- structurally equal referents remain distinct;
- a relation referent may be described without executing it; and
- `:=` definition adds denotation without creating a state transition.

The resulting corpus is the bounded M0 evidence set, not closure over future
surface, Store, evaluator, or runtime work. Relation-schema precedence/import
coverage, longer lineage chains, transition phases and merges, external event
ordering, host adapters, async/cancellation, persistence, and target-specific
realization remain later milestones.

## 7. Post-M0 atomic kernel migration

After M0 is public, the constitutional kernel migration is one serial edge and
one atomic public checkpoint:

```text
kernel identity/schema/relational form/model/revision/delta + canonical v6/v4 wire
  -> derive/execution/intervention/diff/request
  -> frontend AST/parser
  -> elaboration
  -> CLI/examples
  -> generated Rust
  -> tests/corpus
```

Semantic-v5 hashes the bytes of the legacy `Type`, `Entity`, `Value`, and
`Variable` encodings. Those identities must be retired, not retained as live
compatibility. The current `Mode` records lookup orientation, not judgment
modality. The current Clause node collapses relational content with its
assertion occurrence, and the current Revision does not persist predecessor,
occurrence, authority, or judgment lineage. The migration must correct those
boundaries atomically rather than assert they already exist.

The atomic gate requires primitive-encoding and semantic-v5 absence; strict
canonical v6/v4 reload; classification/definition/equality/transition/open-world
distinctions; exact Delta lineage and hospital result parity; and byte-identical
source-deleted generated-Rust results.

The next safe action after review is publication of this atomic M0 successor.
M1 parser and semantic-core redesign remain forbidden until this contract and
corpus are public.
